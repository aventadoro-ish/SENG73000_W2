CAN Communication Circuit (Revision 1)

Began designing the CAN communication circuit for the custom PCB using the Texas Instruments SN65HVD230 CAN transceiver. This device was selected because it operates from a 3.3 V supply, making it directly compatible with the ESP32-S3 while implementing the physical layer of the CAN bus.

The initial schematic focused on establishing the basic communication interface by connecting the transmit (TXD) and receive (RXD) signals between the ESP32-S3 and the transceiver, along with the CANH and CANL differential bus outputs.

Design Decisions

  • Added a PWR_FLAG symbol to indicate that the 3.3 V rail is powered externally, eliminating ERC (Electrical Rules Check) power warnings within KiCad.
  • Placed a 100 nF decoupling capacitor directly across the transceiver's VCC and GND pins. This capacitor acts as a local bypass capacitor, filtering high-frequency noise and providing transient current during switching.
  • Included a NUP2105L transient voltage suppressor (TVS) diode to protect the CANH and CANL differential pair from electrostatic discharge (ESD) and voltage transients that may occur on the CAN bus.
  • Added optional split termination components consisting of two 62 Ω resistors and a 4.7 nF capacitor (currently marked DNP – Do Not Populate). These components were included so the board can support split termination if required during testing.
  • Added screw terminal connectors to simplify wiring between the intermediate elevator controller boards, allowing CAN communication to continue both upward and downward through the elevator network.

Split Termination Research

While a traditional CAN network typically uses a single 120 Ω termination resistor, I researched split termination techniques. Split termination replaces the 120 Ω resistor with two 62 Ω resistors and a capacitor connected to ground. This approach helps reduce common-mode noise, improves EMC performance, and provides additional filtering on the differential pair.

References

  • MEMS Magazine, The Significance of CAN Bus Split Termination
