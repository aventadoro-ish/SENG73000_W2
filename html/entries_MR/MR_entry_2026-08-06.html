Team PCB Update

I prepared an update for the team summarizing the PCB assembly status, initial test results, remaining work, and project costs:

  • Nick and I reflowed three Service PCBs populated with all modules for the Car Controller configuration.
  • The fuse footprint had not been updated, making the 1812 PTC fuses difficult to install on the 0805 pads. The fuse positions on the distributed boards would therefore be shorted.
  • A BOM configuration error left one of the three load-cell module component sets incomplete. I placed a supplementary order for the missing components, which was expected before the end of the week.
  • DigiKey initially shipped only one of the three ordered 5 V regulator ICs. The remaining two arrived in an overnight shipment, and I planned to install them when time allowed because their bottom thermal pads would be difficult to solder manually.
  • Initial testing confirmed that the system-bus and CAN 5 V power OR-ing circuits, the digital 3.3 V regulator, and ESP32-S3 programming all operated correctly.
  • I planned to assemble and test at least one I/O PCB by the end of Sunday, then test the 5 V regulator, GPIO expanders, audio amplifier, and, if practical, the accelerometer.
  • Because the end of the semester was heavily loaded with submissions and presentations, I proposed demonstrating the existing functional platform alongside a flat-packed custom PCB test instead of modelling and installing the new PCBs on the elevator.
  • I proposed completing the remaining Service PCB assembly collaboratively and offered to solder the remaining I/O PCBs.
  • The original DigiKey order cost CAD 511.77, while the JLCPCB order and duties cost CAD 81.69. This produced a shared cost of CAD 197.82 per person. The optional supplementary DigiKey order cost CAD 34.11 and increased the per-person amount to CAD 209.19 if shared.
